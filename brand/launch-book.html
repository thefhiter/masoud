The name

Masoud means the lucky one

مسعود is an old, warm Tunisian name: the happy one, the one luck follows. Everyone has an uncle or a neighbor called Masoud. A robot with that name feels like family from the first day, and the tagline writes itself: with Masoud on your desk, every day is mas3oud.

First run

Six steps, no app to install

Setup happens on Masoud's face and in the phone's browser. He makes his own Wi-Fi, so it works on any phone and needs no app store.

  1. Plug in

    USB-C, no battery. His eyes open and he says hello, then shows a QR code on his screen.

    "Aslema! Ena Masoud."

  2. Scan the QR

    The phone joins his setup Wi-Fi, Masoud-4F2A, and the setup page opens by itself.

  3. Pick a language

    Derja, Français, English or العربية. He switches voice and face captions at once.

  4. Join home Wi-Fi

    Choose the network and type the password. He confirms with a happy face.

  5. Choose his mind

    Local, Cloud or Pet mode. The three options are below. You can change it later from the same page.

  6. Meet him

    Type your name, say "Ya Masoud!" to test the wake word, then shake him once.

    "Dawkha! Mrigel, nharek mas3oud, Sami."

What's inside

A small body and a mind that can live anywhere

The robot handles the face, ears, voice and senses. The heavy thinking happens wherever you chose to put his mind, connected over Wi-Fi.

Mic hears "Ya Masoud"→Wake word→Speech to text→ His mind: Local or Cloud→Reply and a mood tag→Voice on the speaker, mood on the face

We already built this chain for the JARVIS assistant (wake word with openWakeWord, speech to text with faster-whisper, streaming replies, text to speech). Masoud's mind app reuses it and adds the mood tag that drives his face.

Where we are today

The face already runs on an Arduino Uno and a 1.8" screen: boot logo, nine moods including the shake reaction, about 20 frames per second, and control over USB serial. Next is moving it to the ESP32-S3 and adding the mic, speaker and shake sensor.

Before you sell the first one

Check the name is free in the INNORPI trademark database and the WIPO Global Brand Database, then register "Masoud" and the logo at INNORPI in classes 9 (devices, software), 28 (toys) and 42 (cloud service).

Grab the handles and domain while they're free: Instagram, TikTok, Facebook, and a .tn or .com domain.

Set up the company (SUARL or SARL) and apply for the Startup Act label for its tax and funding benefits.

Check the rules for a Wi-Fi device: radio type approval in Tunisia, and CE marking before selling in Europe.

Write a plain privacy notice for Cloud mind and the setup page, and check your obligations with the INPDP before running Masoud Cloud.

Ask a Tunisian business lawyer to review the list above. This book is a plan, not legal advice.
